# Commercial direction and falsifiable pilot

Decision date: 26 September 2026. These are proposed operating choices and internal validation targets, not proven market outcomes.

## Product position

Workwork's enduring mission is to make AI useful in operating a small or midsize company. Quick View is the focused entry product: **a manufacturing owner's question becomes current team evidence, a documented decision, assigned follow-through, and a reported result.** The existing Workwork Cloud product remains the separate longer-term evidence/process foundation.

First customer: an owner-led manufacturer with a recurring delivery coordination problem, one operations coordinator, and approximately 5–15 people who can answer across sales, production, quality, purchasing or logistics. Company size is a prospecting hypothesis, not a market boundary. Select a pilot because the recurring problem is real and the owner will participate, not because the company is willing to try any AI tool.

First paid job: **Which deliveries need my intervention this week, and did last week's decisions happen?** Questions about general strategy, HR, compensation, confidential contracts, autonomous planning and whole-company performance are outside the initial promise. The current shared-company permission model is suitable only where participants are authorized to see the operations evidence.

## What we should sell

Offer a recurring management workflow with a clear outcome:

- One owner and an operations coordinator establish the question, relevant scope and expected response window.
- Staff give brief facts, changes, uncertainty and decisions needed, with reusable evidence.
- The owner sees unresolved requests, dated evidence and action outcomes.
- The next check-in reuses the plan without silently treating prior answers as new facts.

The first subscription should be per company/workflow, with a reasonable included contributor allowance. A strict seat tax can discourage collecting evidence from occasional participants. Set explicit capacity limits and charge separately for substantial integration or bespoke consulting if demand emerges. This is a packaging proposal; no billing is implemented or payment requested by the app.

Test one clearly disclosed price before a pilot ends. A candidate hypothesis is **₩190,000 per company per month for one recurring operating workflow and up to 15 contributors**, subject to checking actual support and model costs. This is a proposed price to discuss with pilot buyers, not market-validated willingness to pay. Do not treat discounted/free participation, compliments or verbal interest as conversion. Do not publish paid promises until account recovery, offboarding, scoped access, backups and support responsibility are operationally ready.

## Four-week pilot after a baseline week

Recruit three manufacturers if available. One friendly company is useful for discovery but insufficient evidence of repeatable demand. Do not contact or enroll companies automatically; the owner of this project chooses actual pilot partners.

| Stage | Activity | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Observe the existing delivery meeting/report/chat routine for one week. | Owner, coordinator and employees' time spent chasing, gathering facts, writing and assembling reports; unresolved decisions; turnaround time. |
| Cycle 1 | Launch one real scoped question with named assignees. | Setup assistance, edits to proposed questions, first response time, total entry effort, whether useful evidence is available when needed. |
| Cycle 2 | Repeat using the reviewed plan; review only changes and open points. | Repeated information employees must retype, explicit rechecks, misunderstanding of dates/scope, use of existing chat links. |
| Cycle 3 | Track one owner-authorized action through a reported result and manager acceptance. | Decision-to-result latency, evidence quality, unresolved obligations, whether acceptance has a substantive reason. |
| Cycle 4 | Owner starts and runs the check-in with minimal founder intervention; present the fixed offer. | Independent repeat use, combined effort versus baseline, purchase decision at the disclosed price, support hours. |

Proposed decision gates (pre-register them with the pilot; they are not industry benchmarks):

- First question launch in less than 10 minutes after enrolling the team, with most proposed requests usable after light edits.
- At least 80% of assigned requests answered within the agreed window, without increasing total reporting effort.
- Median active response entry at most two minutes; separately record time needed to obtain the underlying facts.
- At least 30% lower combined chasing/report-assembly time for comparable cycles. Never claim this reduction from app clicks alone.
- Every material conclusion traceable to a response; no fabricated outcome or hidden missing evidence.
- At least three of four cycles run without the founder personally chasing participation.
- At least two of three owners choose a paid continuation at the previously disclosed price; actual payment is stronger evidence than agreement.

Small pilot samples cannot establish a market-wide success rate. Use the failures to decide what to change, rather than treating thresholds as a prediction.

## Measurement definitions

- **Time to usable answer:** owner-recorded time when the evidence was sufficient for the specific decision, minus question launch. It is not time to 100% response coverage.
- **Total coordination cost:** owner + coordinator + contributor time spent obtaining, entering, rewriting, chasing and assembling the relevant facts. Include work done outside the app.
- **Decision follow-through:** explicitly assigned actions with a reported result and later owner assessment. A checked status is not proof of business benefit.
- **Retention:** owner-initiated comparable weekly cycles and later paid continuation, not number of employee logins.
- **Quality:** traceable evidence, clear uncertainty, preserved scope/dates, corrections that remain visible and appropriate treatment of contradictory sources.
- **Service economics:** monthly subscription revenue minus attributable model/hosting costs, ongoing support labor, and amortized onboarding labor. Record actual costs; do not assume SaaS margins while doing repeated consulting.

The application does not collect covert per-employee effort telemetry or generate employee rankings. Use a simple agreed pilot interview/time log for effort and usefulness. Product timestamps can corroborate sequence and latency but cannot establish human labor saved.

## Prioritized next gates

1. **This release:** decision/action follow-through, explicit evidence-age policy, rechecking without rewriting, repeated check-in drafts, owner exception view and direct authenticated links. Preserve truthful guided/AI mode labeling.
2. **Before a paid pilot with real organization data:** Korean UI and verified phone use; account recovery and operational verification of the implemented member-offboarding flow; appropriate scoped permissions; consistent offsite backup and restore runbook. Add only the communication channel the pilot actually uses. Do not use confidential executive questions under company-wide sharing.
3. **Once repeated value is observed:** configure and evaluate live model drafting on de-identified manufacturing questions; measure edit burden and unsupported assertions against the guided baseline. Model fluency is not an outcome metric.
4. **After retention and paid conversion:** selected read-only source integration, structured KPI measurements with valid units/periods, operational relationship discovery where evidence supports it.

Do not make full ontology design, broad ERP/MES integration, automatic causal attribution or general process mining prerequisites for the first sale. Do not promise them as existing capabilities.

## Stop or change direction when

- Owners do not have a recurring question worth answering.
- Employees see an extra reporting task without an offsetting benefit.
- The founder must repeatedly run the customer's workflow or redesign every question.
- Owners do not use the resulting evidence to decide, confirm or follow up.
- Buyers only want the existing spreadsheet/messenger report copied into another interface.
- Source quality is too weak for the promised decision, or permission boundaries do not match the use case.
- Nobody pays the disclosed offer after a useful, bounded pilot.

Respond to the specific failure: narrow the workflow, reduce input effort, meet users in their existing channel, improve provenance, or revise the offer. Adding more AI-generated content is not automatically the remedy.
